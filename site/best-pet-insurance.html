How this comparison is built

There is no lab test for "best pet insurance". What we can do honestly is put the figures each insurer publishes on its own website side by side, keep the condition attached to every figure, and link to the page it came from. Nothing on this page is estimated, scored or taken from a third-party comparison site.

All figures were read from the brands' own pages on 2026-09-25 and are repeated with their conditions in the fact table below. Where an official page does not publish a figure, the cell says not published on the official site instead of a guess.

The three brands quote their prices under different conditions - different deductibles, annual limits and reimbursement rates - so a raw price-to-price comparison would be misleading. That is why this page does not crown a single winner.

What each plan is best for, on the published facts

Lemonade, if you want the lowest published starting price. Its official cost guide lists policies for dogs and cats starting at $10/month, publishes on-average figures of about $48/month for dogs and about $27/month for cats, five deductible options ($100-$1,000) and three official discounts: multi-pet, bundling with other Lemonade policies, and paying annually instead of monthly. A quote is advertised as completable in as little as 90 seconds, and cancelling within the first 30 days gets you a refund. Source: lemonade.com cost guide and lemonade.com/pet-insurance, checked 2026-09-25.

Spot, if published claim speed and a money-back window matter most. Spot states claims are reimbursed in 48 hours or less, with most processed in under 2 business days (its own admin data, 2019-2024), reimburses up to 90% of covered vet bills, offers a 30-day money-back guarantee (not available in NY; 15 days in ME, LA and WA), has no upper age limit and no vet exam to enrol, and gives 10% off every pet enrolled after the first. Source: spotpet.com, spotpet.com/sample-policy and spotpet.com/cat-insurance, checked 2026-09-25.

Fetch, if you want the most transparent pricing and bundled extras. Fetch publishes breed-level averages on its price page - $35/month for dogs and $22/month for cats on average, French Bulldog $71/month, Golden Retriever $50/month, Domestic Shorthair $24/month - with the methodology stated (a $10,000 annual limit, $300 deductible and 80% reimbursement, 07.01.2024-06.30.2025). It also covers sick-visit exam fees up to $250, includes 24/7 online vet visits up to $1,000/year at no extra cost, states waiting periods of up to 15 days and says claims are typically processed in less than 10 days. Veterans and active military get 10% off every month for life. Source: fetchpet.com price page and fetchpet.com, checked 2026-09-25.

How to choose without a fake ranking

Run the same exercise the brands run themselves: request a quote for your pet on each official site, then compare the quotes you receive against each other, not against the advertised figures on this page. Advertised figures carry conditions - Spot's "from $15/month" assumes an 80% reimbursement rate, a $500 deductible, a $2,500 annual limit and a specific 2-year-old dog in ZIP 32009 - and your pet will not match those conditions exactly.

Three checks that change the outcome more than the brand name: (1) the waiting period before coverage starts - 14 days, 14 days or up to 15 days here; (2) whether a condition your pet already has counts as pre-existing - none of the three covers pre-existing conditions, though Spot publishes a 180-day symptom- and treatment-free rule for curable conditions; (3) the reimbursement percentage and deductible you choose, which move the premium more than the headline price.

Best pet insurance FAQ

What is the best pet insurance in 2026?

There is no single best pet insurance - the honest answer depends on which published fact you care about. Lemonade publishes the lowest starting price (from $10/month), Spot publishes the fastest claim payment (48 hours or less, based on Spot admin data 2019-2024) and Fetch publishes the most detailed pricing (breed-by-breed averages with a stated methodology). Compare quotes for your own pet on each official site, because advertised prices are quoted under different deductible, limit and reimbursement conditions.

Source: https://www.lemonade.com/pet/insurance-guide/pet-insurance-cost/ · checked 2026-09-25

Which pet insurance is cheapest?

The lowest published starting prices are Lemonade at $10/month for dogs and cats, Spot at $15/month for dogs and $9/month for cats, and Fetch, which does not publish an entry price but publishes averages of $35/month for dogs and $22/month for cats. These figures are not directly comparable: Spot's price assumes an 80% reimbursement rate, a $500 deductible and a $2,500 annual limit for a specific dog in ZIP 32009, while Fetch's average assumes a $10,000 annual limit, a $300 deductible and 80% reimbursement. Your real price comes from a quote on the brand's official site.

Source: https://spotpet.com/ · checked 2026-09-25

Is there a real pet insurance promo code?

Not from these three brands. Their official sitemaps were checked on 2026-09-25 and none contains a promo code or coupon page. The discounts that do exist on the official sites are Lemonade's multi-pet, bundle and annual-payment discounts, Spot's 10% multi-pet discount and its group/employee discount, and Fetch's 10% military discount plus Fetch Deals member savings. Third-party coupon sites are not a source we use.

Source: https://www.lemonade.com/sitemap.xml · checked 2026-09-25

How long are pet insurance waiting periods?

Published waiting periods: Lemonade 14 days for illnesses and 30 days for orthopedic conditions (preventative package benefits start the day after purchase); Spot typically 14 days, with state-specific variations detailed in its sample policy; Fetch up to 15 days for accident and illness coverage. Coverage for anything that shows signs before those periods end counts as pre-existing.

Source: https://www.lemonade.com/pet-insurance · checked 2026-09-25

Does pet insurance cover pre-existing conditions?

No. All three brands state that pre-existing conditions are not covered. Spot additionally publishes that a curable condition which has been symptom-free and treatment-free for 180 days is no longer considered pre-existing and can become eligible. Fetch counts anything that occurred or showed clinical signs before enrolment, during the waiting period, or before the pet's first exam as pre-existing.

Source: https://spotpet.com/ · checked 2026-09-25

Do I need a vet exam to buy pet insurance?

Spot states no vet exam is needed for enrolment and has no upper age limit, with puppies and kittens 8 weeks and older eligible. Fetch covers dogs and cats 6 weeks and older and advises enrolling early so conditions are not later classed as pre-existing. For Lemonade, an enrolment exam requirement is not published on the official pages checked on 2026-09-25.

Source: https://spotpet.com/ · checked 2026-09-25
