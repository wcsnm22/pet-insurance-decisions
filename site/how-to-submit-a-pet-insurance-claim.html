How to submit a pet insurance claim, step by step

Quick answer: all three brands run on a reimbursement model, so you pay the vet first and then file. What changes is the clock and the paperwork - Lemonade wants the claim in its app within 180 days of treatment (90 days for Texas policies), Spot allows 270 days from the date of treatment, and Fetch allows 90 days and insists the invoice shows a zero balance. The documents are nearly identical everywhere: an itemized paid invoice plus your pet's medical records. The published speeds are different too - Lemonade says 50% of eligible claims are processed instantly by its AI and most others within 5 business days, Spot says claims are reimbursed in 48 hours or less, and Fetch says claims are typically processed in less than 10 days.

The three workflows, step by step

Lemonade: open the app, tap the pink 'File a Claim' button, answer AI Jim's questions (starting with a truthfulness pledge), describe what happened, record a short video of the incident, upload the invoice and receipt plus records, and submit. Payment is wired to your bank account if the claim is approved.

Spot: pay the vet, sign in to your Member Center and submit the claim online; you have 270 days from the date of treatment. Spot emails you at receipt, if it needs more information, and when the claim is complete.

Fetch: pay the vet first, then upload a photo of the paid invoice in the app (or submit on the member portal) together with the medical records. The invoice must show a zero balance. Set up direct deposit early - Fetch says it pays 5 to 10 days faster than paper checks.

What usually slows a claim down

Three official sources point at the same bottlenecks. First, waiting periods: Lemonade publishes 14 days for illness and 30 days for orthopedic conditions, Spot's sample policy shows a 14-day illness waiting period, and Fetch publishes up to 15 days with a 6-month wait for knee and hip issues - a claim filed before those end is not eligible. Second, missing records: Fetch requires records from your pet's most recent checkup and Lemonade requires a record from within 12 months of the policy start, so a first claim often waits on the vet's paperwork. Third, invoice wording: Fetch will only accept an invoice showing a zero balance or paid in full.

On the appeal side, only Lemonade and Spot publish a route - Lemonade through the app with extra documentation from your vet, Spot by a written email that takes around 30 days to review. Fetch's official pages do not publish one.

FAQ

How long do I have to file a pet insurance claim?

The official windows are different for each brand: Lemonade says within 180 days of treatment (90 days if your policy is in Texas), Spot says within 270 days from the date of treatment, and Fetch says within 90 days of your pet's treatment. Each figure is linked in the table above, so check the one for your provider before you delay filing.

Source: https://www.lemonade.com/pet/insurance-guide/how-to-file-a-pet-insurance-claim/ · checked 2026-09-25

Do I have to pay the vet before filing a claim?

Yes - all three brands use a reimbursement model. You pay the vet bill up front, then file; Lemonade subtracts your deductible and applies co-insurance before wiring the payout, Spot reimburses eligible expenses after your annual deductible is met, and Fetch pays you back after approval and your deductible, by direct deposit.

Source: https://www.lemonade.com/pet/insurance-guide/how-to-file-a-pet-insurance-claim/ · checked 2026-09-25

What documents do I need to submit a pet insurance claim?

An itemized paid invoice plus your pet's medical records. Fetch is the strictest on paper: the invoice must show a zero balance or paid in full, and it wants detailed records from the most recent checkup (SOAP notes). Spot asks for the bill and contacts your vet directly only if records are needed, though sending them may speed things up. Lemonade asks for the vet invoice or paid receipt and the records from that visit.

Source: https://www.fetchpet.com/faqs/claim-submission · checked 2026-09-25

How long does reimbursement take?

Published speeds differ: Lemonade says 50% of eligible claims are processed instantly by AI Jim and most others within 5 business days; Spot says claims are reimbursed in 48 hrs or less; Fetch says claims are typically processed in less than 10 days, and its FAQ adds that processing typically takes within 15 days from when all documents are received. Direct deposit with Fetch is 5 to 10 days faster than paper checks.

Source: https://www.fetchpet.com/claims · checked 2026-09-25

Can I file a claim without an app?

With Lemonade, no - the official guide states the claims process is app-based. Fetch lets you file either in the app or on a computer through the member portal (Claims, then Submit a claim). Spot lets you submit the claim online through your Member Center and also publishes a claims form PDF for download.

Source: https://www.lemonade.com/pet/insurance-guide/how-to-file-a-pet-insurance-claim/ · checked 2026-09-25

What if my claim is denied?

Lemonade's official guidance is to review the denial explanation in the app and then appeal through the app with additional documentation from your vet. Spot requires the appeal in writing, emailed to service@customer.spotpetins.com with your reasons and supporting documents; reviews take around 30 days and the appeal window varies by state. Fetch does not publish an appeal process on its official site as of the check date.

Source: https://spotpet.com/faqs · checked 2026-09-25

Will filing a claim raise my pet insurance rate?

Lemonade publishes that filing claims will never result in a rate change to your Lemonade Pet policy. Neither Spot nor Fetch publishes a matching statement on its official pages as of 2026-09-25, so for those two the answer is not published on the official site - check your policy terms.

Source: https://www.lemonade.com/pet/insurance-guide/lemonade-pet-renewals/ · checked 2026-09-25
